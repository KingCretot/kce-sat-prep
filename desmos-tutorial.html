Week 1 · How to Use Desmos on the SAT

Course page
Step 1 of 10 · Meet the calculator

Your test-day calculator

The digital SAT has this exact calculator built in on every Math question. The left side is the expression list, where you type. The right side is the graph.

Try it in Desmos
  1. Click line 1 of the expression list.
  2. Type 2^10 and look at the answer that appears under it.
Good to know: + and − on the graph zoom in and out. The × on a line deletes it. The keyboard button at the bottom left opens a math keypad with √, π and more.
What answer does Desmos show for 2^10?
Step 2 of 10 · Graph a line

Read the intercepts

When a graph is selected, Desmos marks the points where it crosses the axes with gray dots. Click a gray dot to see its coordinates. If the dots disappear, click the line again.

Try it in Desmos
  1. On a new line, type y=3x-6
  2. Click the line so it is highlighted, then click the gray dot where it crosses the x-axis.
What is the x-intercept of y = 3x − 6? (Type just the x-value.)
Step 3 of 10 · Systems of equations

Find where two lines meet

The solution to a system of two equations is the point where their graphs intersect. Desmos puts a gray dot right on it.

Try it in Desmos
  1. Type y=2x+1 on one line.
  2. Type y=-x+7 on the next line.
  3. Click one of the lines, then click the gray dot where the two lines cross.
SAT tip: if an equation is not in y = form (like 2x + 3y = 12), type it exactly as written. Desmos graphs it anyway.
The system has one solution (x, y). What is y?
Step 4 of 10 · Quadratics

Vertex and solutions in one look

For a parabola, Desmos marks the vertex (the highest or lowest point) and the x-intercepts (the solutions, or zeros) with gray dots.

Try it in Desmos
  1. Type y=x^2-6x+5 (after the 2, press the right arrow to come down from the exponent).
  2. Click the curve, then click the gray dot at the bottom of it.
  3. Click the two gray dots on the x-axis.
What is the minimum value of y = x² − 6x + 5?
Step 5 of 10 · Solve an equation

Type the whole equation

You can type an equation in one variable exactly as the question gives it. Desmos draws the answer as a vertical line at the solution.

Try it in Desmos
  1. Type 2x+3=5x-9
  2. Read where the vertical line crosses the x-axis. Zoom in with + if you need a closer look.
Another way: graph each side on its own, y=2x+3 and y=5x-9, and click where they cross. The x-value is the solution.
What value of x solves 2x + 3 = 5x − 9?
Step 6 of 10 · Sliders

Find a missing constant

When a question has an unknown constant, type it as a letter. Desmos offers a slider for it. Move the slider until the graph does what the question says.

Try it in Desmos
  1. Type the point (2,11) on its own line.
  2. Type y=4x+b and click the b button to add a slider.
  3. Drag the slider until the line passes through the point.
Faster: click the slider's number and type a value straight in.
The line y = 4x + b passes through (2, 11). What is b?
Step 7 of 10 · Tables and regression

Fit a line to data

For a table of values or a line of best fit, use a table and a regression. Desmos finds the equation for you.

Try it in Desmos
  1. Click the + at the top left and choose table.
  2. Fill in x₁: 1, 2, 3, 4 and y₁: 5, 7, 9, 11.
  3. On a new line, type y_1~mx_1+b (the ~ key is next to the 1 on most keyboards; hold Shift).
  4. Read m and b under the regression.
Typing tip: y_1 makes the small 1 (a subscript). Press the right arrow to leave the subscript.
What is the slope m of the line of best fit?
Step 8 of 10 · Inequalities

See the solution region

Desmos shades inequalities. The solution of a system of inequalities is where the shadings overlap.

Try it in Desmos
  1. Type y<2x+1
  2. Type y>-x+4
  3. Type each answer choice as a point, like (3,2), and see which one sits in the overlap.
Which point is a solution to the system y < 2x + 1 and y > −x + 4?
Step 9 of 10 · Settings

Degrees or radians

SAT trigonometry questions are usually in degrees. Desmos starts in radians, so switch it before any trig question that uses degrees.

Try it in Desmos
  1. Click the wrench (graph settings) at the top right of the graph.
  2. Choose Degrees.
  3. Type sin(30)
Also in the wrench menu: show or hide the grid. The house button resets the view if you get lost.
In degree mode, what does Desmos show for sin(30)?
Step 10 of 10 · Use it wisely

When Desmos saves time, and when it doesn't

Desmos is fastest for systems, quadratics, data and inequalities, and for checking an answer. For one- or two-step problems, your pencil is usually faster.

Test-day habits: clear old lines with × before a new question (or start a fresh line), type carefully with parentheses, and check that the graph matches the question before you choose.
